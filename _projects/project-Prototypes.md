## Background

At NDS Denmark we made casual games for OpenTV on behalf of various big TV customers, eg. Nickelodeon, Cartoon Network, etc.

Each game was described in detail in the contract before development started.

## The problem

These written descriptions tended, especially towards the end of a project, to trigger nitpicking and arguments with customers.

Somehow, the wording became more important than the games themselves.

> *Customer: "The descriptions says there should be 5 extra weapons"*
>
> *Us: "The game works great with 4. We can make the whole game better by spending our time in other areas."*
>
> *Customer: "We want our promised extra weapons!"*
>
> *Us: "Yes yes."*

## The idea

I took the initiative to move the company away from written game descriptions and towards prototypes instead.

We still had contracts, but they no longer contained a written description of the game. The prototype took its place.

## How it worked

- We built a prototype of the game in just a few days using GameMaker 8.1.
- The customer played it and gave feedback.
- We iterated on the prototype until we were both happy with it.
- Then we implemented the game in OpenTV. This final implementation normally took around three months.
- Our vast experience with the OpenTV platform made sure we only made prototypes that could successfully translate. 

## Results

Better games, and better relationships with our customers.
